Framework Reference

Security Framework Glossary

Reference guide to the security frameworks, standards, and legal precedents used throughout this knowledge base.

OWASP LLM Top 10 · 2025 Open Web Application Security Project
owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications
The OWASP LLM Top 10 identifies the most critical security risks specific to Large Language Model applications. The 2025 edition covers: LLM01 Prompt Injection, LLM03 Training Data Poisoning, LLM04 Model Denial of Service, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM06 Excessive Agency, LLM10 Model Theft. Each control in this knowledge base maps to one or more LLM categories.
MITRE ATLAS™ Adversarial Threat Landscape for AI Systems
atlas.mitre.org — Maintained by MITRE Corporation
MITRE ATLAS is a knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques against AI/ML systems — the AI equivalent of MITRE ATT&CK. Key techniques referenced: AML.T0043 Craft Adversarial Data, AML.T0020 Poison Training Data, AML.T0024 Exfiltration via ML Inference API, AML.T0018 Backdoor ML Model, AML.T0051 LLM Prompt Injection, AML.T0049 Denial of ML Service.
NIST AI RMF 1.0 AI Risk Management Framework
nist.gov/artificial-intelligence — National Institute of Standards and Technology
The NIST AI RMF provides a structured approach to managing risks throughout the AI system lifecycle. Four core functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE. Key controls: TE.1.4 Robustness/Resilience, TE.1.2 Data Quality & Integrity, SP.3.1 Security, GV.6.1 Supply Chain Risk. Provides the primary organisational framework for implementing mitigations in this assessment.
Moffatt v. Air Canada · 2024 AI Legal Liability Precedent
British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal · Case No. BC-2024
The tribunal ruled that Air Canada was liable for commitments made by its AI chatbot even when they contradicted published policies. The court explicitly rejected Air Canada's argument that the chatbot was a "separate legal entity." This precedent establishes that organisations are liable for AI's negligent misrepresentations — directly informing T5 (Goal Hijacking) risk assessment.
PCI DSS v4.0 Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard
pcisecuritystandards.org — Effective March 2024
Governs the security of cardholder data environments including in-store payment terminals (L4.1) and edge processing nodes (L5.1/L5.2). Non-compliance carries severe financial penalties and may result in loss of card processing privileges. Directly relevant to T7 (Edge AI Hijacking) where offline fraud exploits L4.1/L5.2 payment infrastructure.
GDPR Art. 5 & CCPA Data Protection Regulations
gdpr-info.eu · California Consumer Privacy Act
GDPR Article 5 mandates data minimisation and purpose limitation — AI systems must not retain or expose personal data beyond stated processing purposes. Article 33 requires notification to supervisory authorities within 72 hours of a personal data breach. CCPA provides analogous rights for California residents. Both are directly engaged by T9 (PII Exfiltration).
